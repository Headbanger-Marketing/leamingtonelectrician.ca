Before work begins

Work backwards from the date the work must finish.

Unlike a home with no garage, a detached house with an attached garage can raise two wiring questions. Its panel-to-garage cable route may cross finished space, and garage lighting may share a circuit with rooms in the house.

Start from the date that cannot move, such as a closing, a move-in or an appliance delivery, and list what must be finished before it. Ask each trade and the contractor which step depends on another.

What should I send if the work must be done by a set date?

Send the date, the reason it cannot move and what is already chosen, such as appliances or a vehicle. The contractor can then say what must be assessed first, but an enquiry does not hold any date.

If the house has an attached garage, should its circuits be planned too?

It often helps to review them together, because a garage may share circuits or a supply route with the house. Ask the contractor whether treating them as one scope changes what must be finished before your date.

How do I compare quotes when the finish date is already fixed?

Compare what each written scope includes, what it leaves to other trades and what it assumes about access. A quote that names the date without describing the scope is not enough to compare.

Who needs to approve the work before a closing or move-in?

That depends on the property and the agreement. The seller, the insurer or a lender may each have a say, so ask early who can authorize the work and who can open the door.

Will my enquiry reserve a date for the contractor to visit?

No. Only a provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor can hold a visit date, once you agree the timing with them. They also settle scope and cost with you and confirm their identity and licence separately.
