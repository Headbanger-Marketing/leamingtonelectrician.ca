A symptom is a starting point, not a diagnosis

Loss of power, flickering, recurring protective-device trips or a damaged device can have different causes. The useful information is what happened, where and when, not a guess made from equipment age. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess the installation within a defined diagnostic scope and explain the identified cause before repair work is approved.

Prepare the scope for your Leamington property

Write down what you found on arrival, which rooms were affected and whether anything was left plugged in or running. Photograph visible damage from a safe distance and touch nothing that looks scorched, wet or warm.

Ask the contractor what information about the time away is useful, who holds the keys, and whether the property can be entered safely before work starts. Ask who must be told if the cause affects a closing.

What should the assessment establish?

A property in Leamington stood empty when an electrical problem was found. How far the problem reaches, from a single circuit to a whole floor, is established before any repair is proposed. Parts of the installation that could not be reached are listed apart from those inspected. The contractor explains the identified cause in plain terms and separates repairs needed now from items that can wait. Because a fixed date is involved, the written scope lists which repairs the date depends on and which steps, such as an insurer visit, run separately.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

What should I do if a breaker has tripped while the house sat empty?

Note which breaker it was and what was running, and do not keep resetting it. Leave it off if it trips again, keep the panel cover closed and let the contractor assess it.

Can the sale or move-in go ahead while a fault is still unresolved?

That is a decision for the people named in the agreement, not for this site. Share the written findings with them, and ask the contractor which items affect safe use of the property.
